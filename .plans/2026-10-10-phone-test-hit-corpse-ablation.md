## Decision and observable problem

Derrick tested Cookie's latest AeroBeat playtest on his phone: in **Test mode without CV**, hitting Flow beats with the sword and spawning corpses dropped the scene below 30 FPS. This shows the CV submission timer is not the only performance issue. He approved a **separate phone hit-versus-no-hit diagnostic after** `.plans/2026-10-10-phone-visual-tracking-shadow-ablation.md` lands. Keep that first page's four modes, production CV scheduling/model, scored Play, immutable releases and the existing GUI route unchanged. Use latest published Git commits, record them in every build and report, and never claim Cookie served-build or phone FPS parity from a host fixture.

Read-only research `e218` traced Flow hit to real/synthetic judgement (`aerobeat-web-gameplay/src/session-coordinator.js`), target feedback (`src/session-render-projection.js`), persistent aftermath projection (`src/gameplay-frame-effects.js`) and PlayCanvas corpse materials/pools (`aerobeat-web-renderer/src/renderer-facade.js`). A hit yields feedback and two clipped corpse halves. The renderer pools corpse entities but may first compile material/shader variants; per-frame event/judgement scans may also cost CPU. **Corpse entities do not cast shadows** (`renderer-facade.js:598`). Native shadows might lower the baseline but are not established as the cause of the hit-time hitch. The current phone workload has no judgements/aftermath, so its shadow comparison cannot reproduce this report. Focused e218 follow-up mapped `projectSessionTargets(events, gameplay, t, index, 180, false, 1)` and `projectAftermathEntries(events, gameplay, t, targets, index, null, null, false)` with the **same immutable events array used to create the index**, session purpose `visual_test`, a note-only `{eventId, result:"hit", shadow:false, committedTimelinePositionMs, tier:"great"}` judgement, and synthetic automatic feedback disabled. Inject only commits at or before `t`: future records can improperly displace live aftermath entries. The true hit feedback lasts 350 ms, live icon fades 80 ms, and corpses persist until eighth-hit eviction/offscreen fade. Without genuine private wrist history, midpoint slice position is an explicit approximation; this tests hit *presentation*, not touch collision/cut scoring. Renderer research `ven7` found that bracketing the facade call with `performance.now()` measures main-thread elapsed work, **not GPU time**. `describe().sceneVisuals.assetInstanceCounts` mixes live/corpse instances by asset ID and does not expose corpse-pool or clipped-material variant-cache counts; report intended aftermath-piece counts from the private diagnostic and corroborate pixels, with variant count explicitly unavailable unless a separate diagnostic-only renderer probe is justified. Dedicated corpse pools/material variants/feedback resources are allocated lazily, so compare first versus later hit windows but never infer shader compilation solely from an FPS dip.
