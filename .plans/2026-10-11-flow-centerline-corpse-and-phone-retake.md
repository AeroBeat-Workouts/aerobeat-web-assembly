## Decision and limits

Derrick accepts deliberately simplifying Flow corpse cuts: every arrow should split **lengthwise down its authored local centerline from the middle of its tail to the middle of its tip**, producing complementary left/right halves. An “any” orb should split across a fixed central diameter. Reuse existing arrow/orb meshes and dedicated aftermath entity pools with a bounded fixed set of clip programs; do not create source variants per scored crossing position or arrow direction. Retain real scored contact for gameplay, judgement and feedback; only its corpse visual stops following exact crossing position. Preserve desaturated arrow part contrast, authored direction and tumbling, distinct live note coloring, two halves and right/left separation. Do not assert *exactly* two GPU programs where material/part/blend permutations require a few fixed programs. Verify or carefully qualify actual prewarm: cloning/updating materials is not proof GPU compilation happened. No unrelated production 8445 deployment. No claimed physical speedup until Derrick reruns the new phone tester.

The prior physical v2 Test pair on a **historical mixed-era build** had native 126 new-variant RAFs with renderer/tick p50 84.5/78.5 ms versus 1,222 visible aftermath/no-new RAFs with 4.5/1.5 ms; off withheld all 12,300 observed halves and ran ~36.59 eligible intervals/s, still below earlier non-hit cadence. This is strong association, not shader/GPU causation or parity with latest source. Source readback after sync reveals latest renderer `5277efa` already introduced signed blade-crossing side-piece masks: with `sliceT`, GLSL uses threshold-dependent local X/Y discards; **without `sliceT`, both signs cover the complete length**. The new implementation must preserve latest authored rotation, part colors and stronger half separation while removing that threshold-dependence. Current PlayCanvas supports per-mesh uniforms but the fixed literal mask is simpler unless tests reveal a need for them.
